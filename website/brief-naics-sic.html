Supplementary Brief

NAICS and SIC Industry-Code Coverage in the NPDES Major-Individual Panel, by State (2005–2025)

How often NPDES permits carry an industry classification code, and why coverage varies sharply by state for NAICS but not for SIC.

Download PDF

Scope and Data

This brief measures how often EPA NPDES permits carry an industry classification code — North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) and the older Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) — and how that coverage varies across states. The universe is the major-individual permit population (individual "NPD" permits flagged major in a given year), reconstructed by year over 2005–2025 with entry and exit allowed. The unit of observation is a permit-year; a permit-year "has NAICS" ("has SIC") if that permit appears at least once in NPDES_NAICS.csv (NPDES_SICS.csv). Coverage figures pool all 139,992 permit-years across the 56 reporting states and territories.

Two properties of the source matter. First, NAICS/SIC presence is a time-invariant permit attribute: the code files carry no date, so within a permit, coverage does not change year to year — the cross-state variation below is structural, not a reporting trend. Second, "coverage" means a code is on file; it does not assess whether the code is correct.

Headline Findings

  • SIC is near-universal: 98.7% nationally, at or near 100% in almost every state. Only ND, VT fall below 90%.
  • NAICS is sparse and sharply state-dependent: 35.9% nationally. The distribution is bimodal, not centered on the mean: 16 states and territories record NAICS on essentially none of their major-individual permits, while 10 record it on 95%+.
  • The gap is administrative, not industrial. Whether a permit has a NAICS code tracks the permitting authority, not the facility. Several of the largest state programs sit at ≈0% (e.g. CA, IL, OH, WI, MD, WV; TX at 0.2%), while neighbors of similar industrial mix (e.g. KY, OK, ID at 99–100%) are fully coded.

General State Reporting of NAICS and SIC Codes

Industry codes enter ICIS-NPDES at permit issuance and are maintained by the administering authority — the state environmental agency in delegated (authorized) states, or the EPA Region otherwise. Because SIC long predates NAICS and was the required classification when most of these permits and their legacy records were created, virtually every major-individual permit carries a SIC code: coverage is 98.7% pooled and effectively saturated in all but a couple of jurisdictions. NAICS adoption, by contrast, was never uniformly back-filled. A state's ICIS-NPDES records either systematically include NAICS (yielding near-100% coverage) or they do not (yielding near-0%), producing the two-cluster pattern in the state table below rather than a smooth gradient. The modest national upward drift over the window — from 33.6% (2005) to 38.9% (2025) in the year table below, against flat SIC — reflects newer permits in already-coded states, not laggard states beginning to report.

Implication for analysis. For any cross-state industry classification, use SIC: its coverage is uniform, so it does not confound industry with state. Pooled NAICS is unusable, and conditioning on NAICS presence would silently select on state (dropping entire programs such as CA, OH, and IL). This is recorded in the project's data documentation as the operative guidance for the panel.

Caveats

  • Figures are permit-year shares pooled over the window; a state's permit count is its total active major-individual permit-years, not distinct permits. Because coverage is time-invariant within a permit, pooling does not distort the shares.
  • Coverage measures presence of a code, not its accuracy or its match to the facility's true activity.
  • "States" includes DC and U.S. territories (56 units); the smallest have very few permit-years, so their percentages are noisy.
  • ND, VT are the only sub-90% SIC jurisdictions and warrant a look before use — atypical against the otherwise saturated SIC pattern.

Reproducibility

Coverage is computed by scripts/diagnostics/naics_sic_coverage_by_state_year.R (writes a state × year CSV to output/tables/). This brief's tables are generated from the newest such CSV (naics_sic_coverage_by_state_year_2026-07-13_1219.csv). Rerun that generator after any data refresh.
